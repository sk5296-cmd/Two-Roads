The Reluctant Leaders

Thousands of years and an ocean apart, George Washington and Moshe Rabbeinu shared a profound paradox: they were the exact leaders their people needed, precisely because they did not want the job.

George Washington

⚔️

The Reluctant Hero

Hover to reveal history

"I do not think myself equal to the Command I am honoured with." Despite arriving in uniform, Washington deeply doubted his own ability to lead the continental forces against the greatest military in the world.

— Washington's address to Congress (McCullough, 1776)

The Unifying Figure

Hover to reveal history

"Perseverance and spirit have done wonders in all ages." He was the indispensable man, the only figure capable of holding together thirteen disparate, arguing colonies into a unified fighting force.

— Gen. Washington's Orders (McCullough, 1776)

The Final Sacrifice

Hover to reveal history

He stepped away from power entirely. "The time is now near at hand which must probably determine whether Americans are to be freemen or slaves." He ensured the nation outlived his individual rule.

— Orders before Long Island (McCullough, 1776)

Moshe Rabbeinu

🔥

The Reluctant Prophet

Hover to reveal history

At the burning bush, he repeatedly attempted to refuse the mantle of leadership, arguing with the Almighty about his own physical and social inadequacies: "Please, my Lord, I am not a man of words... for I am heavy of mouth and heavy of speech."

— Shemot / Exodus 4:10

The Unifying Figure

Hover to reveal history

Tasked with taking a fractured population of former slaves, prone to rebellion and despair, and forging them into a unified, covenantal nation at Sinai: "And you shall be to Me a kingdom of princes and a holy nation."

— Shemot / Exodus 19:6
🌅

The Final Sacrifice

Hover to reveal history

He led the Israelites for 40 grueling years through the desert, only to be denied entry himself so the people would rely on the Law, not the man. "I have let you see it with your eyes, but you shall not cross over there."

— Devarim / Deuteronomy 34:4
